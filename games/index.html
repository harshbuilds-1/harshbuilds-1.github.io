Dev notes

What I learned making them.

One lesson from each of four different games, with the specific rule or bug that taught it.

From Tidebound

Teach the rule by letting players try.

The game starts at high tide with the goal in plain sight. Players try to swim, tire out, and wash back to the lantern. Thirty seconds later the sandbar rises out of the sea and nobody needs a tutorial to know what to do.

From Soap Panic

"Stop" needs a precise definition.

To finish, the duck has to stay inside the ring for 1 second, within 0.78 units of the centre and moving slower than 0.9 units per second. Going too fast resets the hold. Those three numbers turned "park the duck" into a skill you can learn.

From Checkpoint Run

"Build succeeded" doesn't mean the game works.

The browser build loaded, but the road and truck were missing. After I fixed it, the bug came back because the host was serving an old build file. Now I always play the published version, not just the editor.

From Last Signal

Bring the information to the player.

Crew drift off screen, so arrows on the screen edge show each one's name, oxygen and distance. Danger is never colour alone: it also pulses and adds a "!". Players decide who to save without searching the map.

01 · Unity 6 · 3D · Chapter 1 of 3 · in progress · Nov 2026

Tidebound

Can one changing rule, the tide, carry a whole level, so that players learn it without a tutorial?

The rules

90 sone tide cycle: low 25 s, rising 20 s, high 25 s, falling 20 s
2.3 mbetween low and high tide; the sandbar sits in between
9 sof swimming stamina: not enough to cross at high tide
0.12 scoyote time and jump buffer, so jumps never feel "eaten"

Designed with MDA

MechanicDynamicAesthetic
Tide cycle with a visible timelinePlayers watch the bar, plan and waitAnticipation, challenge
Sandbar only dry at low tideTry early, fail, come back at low tideDiscovery
Swimming uses staminaSwim out, turn back or get washed backGentle tension
No death: washed back to the last lightExperiment without fearCalm, fairness
Lighting the beacon wakes the lighthouse and moves the sun to golden hourStop and lookFulfilment

Readability

  • Warm gold always means a goal or safety: beacons, lanterns, the goal marker
  • Wet, darker sand shows where the sea will come back to; a foam line marks the waterline
  • Tall red-banded posts mark the sandbar, so the path reads even under water
  • The tide bar shows the whole cycle, so players can see what's next, not just what's now

Built

The island, props, character, UI and sounds are all generated from code. The water and shoreline are hand-written shaders that read the tide height, so foam always lines up with the sea. The character uses procedural animation and a Verlet-rope scarf. The web build is 7.3 MB.

Bugs that taught me something

  • Everything looked washed out: vertex colours were in gamma while the project renders in linear colour
  • The flat sandbar turned solid white at the waterline, so the foam became a thin, patchy band
  • A very low dusk sun caused striped "shadow acne", fixed with a higher sun and shadow bias

02 · Unity 6 · vertical slice · Nov 2026

Last Signal

Can a HUD help a player make fast, fair decisions about who to save first?

The rules

5astronauts, each with their own oxygen (62–120 s)
8 mbeam range, and the ship must be under 9 m/s to lock
1.1 sof steady beam to pull someone aboard
3hull points; only hits above 7 m/s hurt

What the HUD does

  • An oxygen ring over each astronaut that empties as time runs out, with the seconds shown
  • Arrows on the screen edge for crew you can't see, with name, oxygen and distance
  • Danger never relies on colour alone: low oxygen turns red, pulses and adds "!"; low hull adds a red screen edge and "HULL CRITICAL"
  • "Too fast, slow down to lock the beam" when you're close but moving too quickly

Playtest → changes

  • Gentle bumps were costing hull, so damage now starts at 7 m/s (was 4)
  • VEGA ran out before a first-time player could read the HUD, so she got +10 s
  • Edge labels were cut off, so they now flip to the side with room
  • A mouse click could leave keyboard and gamepad players stuck in menus; pressing a direction now re-selects a button

Built

Everything except the camera and light is built from code at runtime: low-poly rocks, the docking ring, the HUD and the menus. All sound is synthesised in code. Glow uses soft additive "halo" sprites instead of a bloom pass. The web build is 7.7 MB.

03 · Unity · DGM6670 course prototype

Soap Panic

How do you make losing control feel funny instead of unfair?

The rules, with real numbers

1.0 shold inside the finish ring
0.78units max from ring centre
< 0.9units/sec to count as parked
0.15 sairborne before it counts as a fall

Course: 5 sections, each teaches one thing

  • Warm up: feel the slide on a wide straight
  • First turn: counter-steer before the turn
  • Narrow crossing: controlled speed beside a moving toothbrush
  • The almost corner: slow down for the bumper
  • Park the duck: approach gently and hold

Built

Rigidbody movement with stronger counter-steering, raycast ground check, round states (ready → racing → falling → finished), separate pause, best time saved with PlayerPrefs, follow/overview camera with impact shake, and generated sound. No countdown or game-over screen: a fall respawns you in about 1.6 seconds, so you want to try again straight away.

Next

A classmate playtest using the questions in my game design document, to find out whether the final stop feels fair.

04 · Unity · personal prototype

Stack & Splat

Can the HUD show the risk before the player fails?

The trade-off

The tower moves forward on its own. Boosting gets you there faster but makes the stack less stable; braking steadies it. The "Steady hands" meter and the height of the stack make that risk visible all the time.

Built

  • Auto-forward movement with steering, boost and brake
  • Kitchen hazards (rolling pins, mugs)
  • Scoring with a saved best score
  • Pause, retry and camera options

Next

Find out whether players notice the balance meter before the stack falls, or only afterwards.

05 · Unity · my first Unity project · Sep 30 – Oct 6, 2026

Checkpoint Run

The course exercise only asked you to drive down a road. How do you turn that into a game?

What I added

3checkpoints required to finish
+6 sper gate, collected only once
≈22 mper second with both movement methods (open road)
Shiftchanges A/D to strafe, but doesn't move you on its own

Bugs I hit

  • Truck flipped: when it was upside down, the detection ray was too short to reach the ground. I adjusted it and tested a deliberate flip.
  • Missing world in WebGL: the UI loaded but the road and truck didn't render. It was a graphics/build settings issue.
  • The bug came back: the host was serving an old build-data file. I cleaned the upload and played it again.

What I learned

Transform.Translate vs Rigidbody.MovePosition, and Input.GetButton for held modifier keys. Most of all: test the published build.

06 · Unity · DGM6670 personal prototype

Last Delivery

Can what the player is holding create the decision?

The loop

Carry the parcel → put it down → hit the switch (only with empty hands) → pick it up again → cross before the gate closes. Carrying also slows you down, and sprinting and jumping only work with empty hands.

100 sper run
12 sthe gate stays open
−8 sfor a fall or a sweeper hit
2checkpoints: you and the parcel respawn there

Feedback & recovery

The HUD shows what you're carrying ("Carrying: slower movement. E puts the parcel down."), the gate has a countdown and signal lights, and there's pause and retry. One change I made: the plain capsule became a courier with a cap, satchel and carrying pose, so the player's state is visible on the character.

07 · Unity · course prototype

Rescue Ranch

What reward makes a player want to keep a streak going?

The mechanic I added

Dodge the incoming animals and throw food to feed them. Consecutive feeds raise your multiplier. Every fifth feed in a chain charges an emergency slow-down you can use with E. The chain panel always shows how many feeds are left until the next charge.

08 · VR · ShapesXR · course escape-room project

The Last Train: Platform Zero

How do you keep a puzzle readable when the player can stand anywhere and look anywhere?

Structure

Three rooms (ticket hall → control room → platform), eight states and one ending. Each puzzle has one correct answer, followed by a separate "continue" press so the player can read the result before moving on. Symbols always come with words, and each room has its own colour.

Iteration

When I reviewed the saved viewpoints, I found a pillar hiding the start of the clue text and status text that was hard to read. I moved larger clue boards in front of the pillars and added dark backings behind the text.
