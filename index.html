Hair by Gurbina

A fuller service story.
A clearer route to the chair.

Reorganizing a salon website so visitors can discover everyday hair services alongside bridal work and find their next step.

My role
Freelance web designer & developer
Focus
Information architecture, mobile UX, frontend
Explore the live website ↗
Read the project notes +

Start with the service, then the next step.

The earlier site emphasized bridal work. The brief was to represent the salon’s broader offer and clarify how someone moves from interest to an appointment.

My contribution

  • Reordered service sections and galleries to make the full offer easier to find.
  • Connected booking calls to action and added mobile WhatsApp access.
  • Organized content around inquiry, booking, consultation, consent, and policies.

Build choices

Converted 24 site images to WebP and updated references. Added a fallback to a content-reveal interaction that could otherwise leave mobile content hidden.

What is visible now

The current site gives colour, cuts, and care a clear opening position, uses a gallery to show the work, and links appointment booking from the navigation and page sections.

What remains to learn

Booking-conversion uplift has not been measured. A useful next study is to observe how first-time visitors find a service, understand the booking route, and locate appointment policies.

Scope: this case study covers my website work. The salon owns its services, photography, and customer relationships; no salon-business results are attributed to the design without evidence.

Project 02 / Academic prototype / UnityPlay in browser ↗

Last Delivery

One parcel. Two hands.
A gate that won’t wait.

A courier must put down a parcel to operate a switch, then collect it and cross before the gate closes. A small interaction becomes a timing and planning challenge.

Context
Personal student prototype, DGM6670
Focus
Interaction states, feedback, checkpoint recovery
Build
Unity · AI-assisted development
Play Last Delivery ↗

Desktop browser and keyboard recommended.

Explore the mechanic & iteration +
Carry parcel→Put down→Open gate→Collect & cross

The design question

Can a small change in what the player is holding create a clear and interesting decision? The switch requires empty hands, so carrying and interacting become distinct states.

Rules the player can read

A run lasts 100 seconds. A gate stays open for 12 seconds. Falling or touching a moving sweeper costs 8 seconds and returns both the player and parcel to a checkpoint.

Feedback & recovery

On-screen prompts explain parcel state. Gate countdowns and signal lights show urgency. Checkpoints, pause, and retry make a failed attempt recoverable.

An implemented iteration

The initial capsule became a courier with a cap, satchel, and walking/carrying poses. A taped parcel, path markings, and gate countdowns make the objects and states more distinct while retaining a simple graybox scene.

My role & assistance

This is my personal student project, developed with ChatGPT/Codex assistance across ideas, coding, visuals, debugging, documentation, and functional testing. I use it to practice explaining and evaluating game decisions.

Current status

The browser prototype is playable. Professor and classmate feedback, a revised design document, and a documented human playtest are the next steps. No measured usability improvement is claimed.

Project 03 / Academic team project / AccessibilityInteractive prototype ↗

Adaptive Legibility

When the background changes,
should the interface respond?

An interactive exploration of translucent interfaces, changing backgrounds, and adaptive text contrast.

My role
Contributor in a three-person team
Focus
Interaction design, contrast evaluation, prototyping
Try the comparison ↗
Read the question, approach & limits +

The question

How can a translucent material respond to changing backgrounds while keeping text legible? The exploration uses contrast as a concrete design constraint.

The approach

Three treatments can be compared under the same conditions. Controls expose background brightness, visual complexity, simulated glare, and viewing conditions.

My contribution

Collaborated in a three-person team on an adaptive-opacity prototype. The working demonstration makes the material response visible and gives the team a shared way to discuss contrast.

Scope & limitations

This is an independent academic simulation, not an Apple product or validation of the actual operating system. Displayed ratios are model outputs. They do not establish real-world reading performance or system-wide accessibility compliance.

02 / Unity lab

Play with a purpose.

Smaller experiments in movement, risk, and feedback. Real prototype screenshots, with local Mac builds.

Movement / Counter-steering

Soap Panic

Getting there is easy.
Stopping is the challenge.

Steer a duck on slippery soap through a bathroom course. Momentum, wobble, and quick retries turn a simple route into a control problem.

Inside the prototype +

Built: force-based movement, falls, a moving toothbrush, best time, pause/retry, and close/overview cameras.

Design focus: course readability and a finish that requires the player to settle in place.

Status: Apple Silicon Mac build. A public web build and human usability study are still to come.

Risk / Reward / Balance

Stack & Splat

More pancakes.
More questionable decisions.

Deliver a wobbling tower through a kitchen. Boosting gains speed and instability; braking helps steady the stack.

Inside the prototype +

Built: auto-forward movement, steering, boost/brake, moving hazards, scoring, saved best score, pause, retry, and camera options.

Design focus: making the trade-off between speed and stability visible through stack height and balance feedback.

Status: Apple Silicon Mac build. Functional checks are complete; audience appeal and usability are unmeasured.

Both experiments were developed with AI assistance in concept, implementation, visuals, and testing. These are learning prototypes. Ask me for a walkthrough ↗
